ROUTERS

A router is a networking device that connects multiple computer networks and manages traffic between them. 
It operates at Layer 3 (Network Layer) of the OSI model, directing data packets to their exact destinations based on IP addresses.

Key Functions
  
  - Data Routing: Uses routing tables and algorithms to determine the most efficient path for a data packet to travel across an internetwork.
  
  - Network Translation: Performs Network Address Translation (NAT) so that multiple local devices can share a single public IP address.
  
  - Traffic Management: Prevents network congestion by isolating traffic and ensuring data flows efficiently between Local Area Networks (LANs) and Wide Area Networks (WANs).
  
  - Security: Many enterprise and consumer routers include built-in firewalls and Quality of Service (QoS) to protect against threats and prioritize important network traffic.
  


Types of Routers

  - Core Routers: Used by service providers or large enterprises to route data within networks, rather than to sources outside the network.
    
  - Edge Routers (Gateway Routers): Sit at the boundary of a network to connect the internal network to external networks (like the Internet).
  
  - Wireless Routers: Commonly used in homes and small offices. They act as an access point to route traffic wirelessly (via Wi-Fi) to and from connected devices.
  
  - Virtual Routers: Software-based routing instances used in highly scalable, cloud, or software-defined networking (SDN) environments.
